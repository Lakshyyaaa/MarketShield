The Noticee had submitted instance wise respon se before the DA to further its arguments . The DA , based on her examination , accepted the explanation for 101 instances and on 151 instances concluded that the Noticee had failed to settle the running accounts of clients. Further, the DA did not make any conclusions with respect to 2 instances. 16. Accordingly, the SCN alleged that the Noticee had violated BSE Notices dated February 10, 2020 and March 31, 2022 read with SEBI Circular dated June 16, 2021 on 151 instances (77+28+46) and elaborated as follows: i. On 77 instances, the Noticee had submitted that it failed to settle the client accounts due to incorrect Bank IFSC. However, the Noticee failed to report the said client accounts as untraceable in its Exchange Reporting and therefore, on such instances the Noticee failed to follow the applicable directions as mandated in SEBI and Exchange Circulars. ii. On 28 instances , the Noticee had incorrectly submitted that the accounts were settled on due date but had actually failed to settle the said accounts as per applicable provisions; and iii. On 46 instance s, the Noticee had incorrectly submitted that the accounts did not require settlement. Regulation 9 (b) and 9 (f) read with Schedule II (Code of Conduct) of the Stock Brokers Regulations, requires the Stock B rokers to ensure compliance with the provisions of the SEBI Act, Rules, Regulations, as well as the circulars and directives issued by SEBI and the stock exchanges. SEBI Circular dated June 16, 2021, through its Clause 5.1, mandates that stock brokers must settle the running accounts of their clients at least once every 30 or 90 days, as per the client’s preference, after considering the end-of-day (EOD) fund obligations across all exchanges on the settlement date .
